# CONTRACT.md — Vector

This is the product Vector is building towards, not a description of what ships today. Every clause has a stable ID, states the behaviour a buyer gets, names the acceptance evidence that proves it through a public door (a browser journey, an HTTP route, a command, a signed webhook, a built mobile app), and carries a status judged from the current code and tests: **done** (implemented and exercised by the cited test), **partial** (some of the behaviour exists at the cited file, the rest is open) or **missing** (no implementation yet). The status table at the end lists every clause. Difficulty — native apps, provider apps, coding-agent sandboxes, live syncs — is recorded as engineering work in a clause, never as a reason to drop it.

Security, privacy and operational requirements are in BASELINE.md, and no product clause supersedes a baseline clause. Capacity and rate limits in seed.json remain estimates; a stated latency is a target until measured. Clauses marked *Policy* name intended replaceable behaviour. Dated evidence distinguishes executed checks from unmet obligations.

## Scope

Vector is a full Linear/Jira-class issue tracker for software teams. A team that runs on Linear, Jira Software or GitHub Issues and Projects — or on the up-and-comers Plane and Huly — can move to Vector and keep doing the whole job: fast keyboard-first issues, triage, cycles, projects, initiatives and roadmaps; roles and private teams; reporting; mobile apps; follow-up automation; and the integrations the category runs on (GitHub, GitLab, Slack, Microsoft Teams, Zendesk, Intercom, Sentry, Figma, Notion and the coding agents). Work flows from report to merged change and on to a verified fix: an accepted issue can be delegated to a coding agent that opens a tested pull request under human review, the deployment is tracked, and the issue closes only when the user-facing problem is shown to be gone.

**Buyer.** A US software team of 5 to 500 people — engineering, product and design, with support and leadership as readers — replacing Linear, a Jira Software project or GitHub Projects. Self-hosted on the buyer's own Cloudflare account, free and open source; money appears only as customer revenue in USD (CUST-005).

**Separation from sibling products.** Vector keeps the market's separation and links to its siblings rather than rebuilding them:

- **Penny** owns the customer feedback board, public roadmap and public changelog (the Canny/Productboard job). Vector receives Penny requests as customer-request links on issues and hands shipped work back to Penny (CUST-001, CUST-003, RELEASE-003).
- **HappyToHelp** owns the support inbox, **Orbit** the CRM, **Spool** error tracking, **Uptime** external checks and **Oncall** paging. Vector links their records to issues and reads their evidence (CUST-001, ERROR-002, PUSH-004) but never replaces them.
- Cross-functional work management for non-software teams (the Asana, monday.com and ClickUp job) is not Vector's product; Vector is for software delivery.
- The code host, CI runners and deploy platform stay GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket and the buyer's CI; Vector owns work state, relationships and delivery evidence.

**Scope before this contract** was one business workspace of equal operators built around evidence-led intake decisions, issues, work streams, projects, dated cycles, saved views, search, in-app notifications and Linear/Jira file imports, with integrations, AI, roadmaps, roles, chat, mobile and real-time collaboration declared out of scope. Those clauses remain where they still hold, rewritten where the scope changed, and now form one part of the full job.

## Workspace, people and permissions

- **OPS-001 — Workspace access.** Every workspace member sees every public team's issues, intake items, cycles, projects, initiatives, labels and shared saved views, and every notification addressed to them, and acts on them as the role matrix (ROLE-001, ROLE-008) allows; team membership organises work and, for a public team, never restricts access. Roles (ROLE-001), private teams (ROLE-002) and guests (ROLE-003) are the only rules that narrow access, and a private saved view (VIEW-002) is visible only to its owner. Existing configured installations retain completion through the additive upgrade. *Acceptance:* `tests/worker/first-run.test.ts` (tests tagged SETUP-001). *Status:* done — `tests/worker/first-run.test.ts`.
- **OPS-002 — Member management.** Members are invited, removed by suspension (OPS-004, ROLE-011), reactivated and erased (ROLE-012) in Settings → Members, and every invitation names the invitee's role (ROLE-001) and, for a Guest, the teams they join (ROLE-003). - **OPS-003 — Workspace time.** Settings hold one IANA workspace timezone. ## Teams and workflows

- **TEAM-001 — Team identity.** A team has a name and an identifier prefix, each unique across archived and non-archived teams. The prefix is two to ten uppercase letters and cannot be changed once the team has an issue, so existing identifiers stay resolvable. Every issue belongs to exactly one team. *Acceptance:* `tests/e2e/admin-team-journey.mjs` (tests tagged TEAM-001). *Status:* done — `tests/e2e/admin-team-journey.mjs`.
- **TEAM-002 — Team workflow.** Every team has an ordered list of workflow statuses with at least one status in each of the Intake, Backlog, Unstarted, Started, Completed, Canceled, and Duplicate categories, and status names unique within the team. Owners, Admins and the team's owners (ROLE-007) can add, rename, and reorder statuses, and so can every member of the team while its *Who manages team settings* permission is All team members (ROLE-009); other Members and all Guests are refused. A status never changes category, and no category is added or removed. A team's *default status* in a category is the first status of that category in workflow order, and every clause naming a category as a destination moves the issue there. *Acceptance:* `tests/e2e/admin-team-journey.mjs`, `tests/worker/base-auth.test.ts` (tests tagged TEAM-002) for the workflow rules, plus the TEAM-002 rows of the role-matrix journey (ROLE-001): a team owner renames a status, and a Member who does not own the team gets 403 under the default permission and succeeds once the permission is All team members. *Status:* partial — `tests/e2e/admin-team-journey.mjs` (workflow editing by any operator under the earlier equal-operator rule; the role restriction is open with ROLE-001 and ROLE-007).
- **TEAM-003 — Team archiving.** Archiving a team stops new issues, intake, and issue moves from targeting it while preserving its issues, cycles, projects, identifiers, and history; a signal source request naming an archived team creates nothing and returns 409. Unarchiving restores normal use. Teams are never deleted; archiving is the only terminal state. *Acceptance:* `tests/e2e/admin-archive-team-journey.mjs` (tests tagged TEAM-003). *Status:* done — `tests/e2e/admin-archive-team-journey.mjs`.
- **TEAM-004 — Status removal.** Removing a workflow status requires a destination status in the same category and atomically moves every issue to it. Vector rejects removal of a category's last status and records the status mapping in audit history. *Acceptance:* `tests/e2e/admin-team-journey.mjs` (tests tagged TEAM-004). *Status:* done — `tests/e2e/admin-team-journey.mjs`.

## Issues

- **ISSUE-001 — Creation.** An operator can create an issue with a required title and team plus an optional Markdown description (written in the ISSUE-026 editor, with inline images and video under ISSUE-024 and checklists under ISSUE-025), assignee, project, cycle, priority, labels, estimate, due date, expected outcome, customer impact, and values for the custom fields defined for its team (FIELD-001, which carries their types and acceptance). It starts in the team's default Backlog status unless another non-Intake status is explicitly chosen. *Policy: `issue.defaults.v1`; default: unassigned, no priority, no cycle, and no project.* *Acceptance:* `tests/e2e/core-runtime.mjs`, `tests/markdown-rendering.test.tsx` (tests tagged ISSUE-001). *Status:* done — `tests/e2e/core-runtime.mjs`.
- **ISSUE-002 — Stable identifiers.** An issue identifier is its team's prefix, a hyphen, and an integer one higher than the highest ever allocated under that prefix, so a number is never reused after an issue is deleted. The identifier never changes, including when the issue moves teams (ISSUE-012); a prefix therefore records where an issue started, not where it lives. *Acceptance:* `tests/worker/issue-deletion-relations.test.ts` (tests tagged ISSUE-002). *Status:* done — `tests/worker/issue-deletion-relations.test.ts`.
- **ISSUE-003 — Priority.** An issue has exactly one priority: Urgent, High, Medium, Low, or No priority. No priority is the default and sorts after Low. *Acceptance:* A test that sets each of the five priorities and checks that No priority is the default and sorts after Low. *Status:* done — `tests/e2e/core-board-journey.mjs`, `evidence/keyboard-board/ISSUE-003/`.
- **ISSUE-004 — Status transitions.** A version-checked operator mutation (ISSUE-006) can move an issue in any direction among the Backlog, Unstarted, Started, Completed, and Canceled statuses of its team's workflow. Vector rejects moving any issue into an Intake status. Leaving Intake uses INTAKE-005, INTAKE-007, or REL-003; entering or leaving Duplicate uses REL-003 or REL-005. Entering Completed records the completion time and entering Canceled records the cancellation time; leaving one of those categories clears the time it recorded and removes no activity. *Acceptance:* `tests/worker/issue-session-projection.test.ts` (tests tagged ISSUE-004). *Status:* done — `tests/worker/issue-session-projection.test.ts`.
- **ISSUE-005 — Assignment.** An issue has at most one assignee. Assigning, reassigning, or unassigning it takes effect immediately and records the actor, the previous assignee, and the new assignee. *Acceptance:* `tests/worker/removal-assignment.test.ts` (tests tagged ISSUE-005). *Status:* done — `tests/worker/removal-assignment.test.ts`.
- **ISSUE-006 — Concurrent edits.** Every issue carries an integer version that increases by one on each mutation that appends an activity entry under ISSUE-007. An issue mutation carries the version the client last read; if the stored version is higher, Vector rejects the mutation with 409 and returns the current issue and version, changing nothing. Adding a comment, evidence revision, follower, or notification does not change the version and is never rejected as stale. *Acceptance:* `tests/e2e/core-runtime.mjs`, `tests/e2e/collaboration-preferences.mjs`, `tests/worker/dependencies.test.ts` (tests tagged ISSUE-006). *Status:* done — `tests/e2e/core-runtime.mjs`.
- **ISSUE-007 — Activity history.** Creation and every change to title, description, team, status, priority, assignee, project, cycle, estimate, due date, labels, expected outcome, customer impact, custom fields, duplicate target, parent, or dependency append an immutable activity entry recording the time, the previous value, the new value, and the actor — the operator, or the named automation when a cycle closure, deferral return, or import acted. *Acceptance:* `tests/worker/dependencies.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/admin-label-journey.mjs`, `tests/worker/cycle-closure.test.ts` (tests tagged ISSUE-007). *Status:* done — `tests/worker/dependencies.test.ts`.
- **ISSUE-008 — Comments, threads and resolution.** Any member who can see an issue (a Guest only in invited teams) can add a Markdown comment in the ISSUE-026 editor, with inline images and video (ISSUE-024) and checklists (ISSUE-025). Each top-level comment starts a thread: members reply to it, and replies show under it in order. Any member who can comment can resolve a thread when its question is answered, which collapses it to one line naming who resolved it and when, and can reopen it; a new reply reopens a resolved thread. A comment can also quote highlighted text of the issue description or a document (PROJ-013) as an inline comment, which anchors the thread to that text and shows it in the margin; if the text is later removed, the thread stays in the issue with the quoted text shown as removed. Only a comment's author edits it; an edited comment shows its edit time. Authors delete their own comments; Owners, Admins and the team's owners delete anyone's (role matrix, ROLE-001); the entry records who acted. Deleting a comment removes its body, attachments, links, and search entry, leaves a tombstone and an activity entry in the thread, keeps its replies, and does not remove the follower state it created. A synced Slack or Teams thread maps to exactly one Vector comment thread (SLACK-002). *Acceptance:* `tests/worker/discussion-access.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/collaboration-runtime.mjs`, `tests/e2e/core-runtime.mjs` (tests tagged ISSUE-008) for comment creation, editing, deletion and tombstones; plus a thread journey that replies twice to a comment, resolves the thread and sees it collapse to one line with the resolver, posts a new reply and sees it reopen, adds an inline comment on highlighted description text and finds it anchored in the margin, and is refused 403 editing another member's comment; plus the SLACK-002 fixture in which one Slack thread's replies land only in its mapped comment thread. *Status:* partial — `tests/worker/discussion-threads.test.ts`, `tests/worker/discussion-access.test.ts`, `tests/e2e/core-threads-journey.mjs`; `evidence/comment-threads/ISSUE-008/` (threads with ordered replies, resolve and reopen naming who and when, reopen on a new reply, author-only editing refused 403, and deletion by the author or an Owner or Admin with tombstones that keep replies are implemented; inline comments quoting description or document text are not built; team-owner deletion waits on ROLE-007, Guest limits on ROLE-003, inline images and video on ISSUE-024, and the Slack thread mapping on SLACK-002).
- **ISSUE-009 — Attachments and links.** Operators can attach files and named HTTPS links to an issue or a comment. Removing either records the action; removing a file also deletes its blob under `BASE-DATA-003`. ### Connecting integrations

Vector runs on each buyer's own Cloudflare account, so there is no shared Slack, GitHub or Teams app that reaches every installation, and Run Edit Run runs no event relay for them: each installation owns its provider apps, created for it by the provider's own app-creation flow, so its events and tokens never pass through a third party. Run Edit Run publishes only the apps whose provider requires a store listing and that call the installation by URL: the mobile and desktop apps with their push relay (MOBILE-004) and the Zapier and Make apps (CONNECT-001).

- **INTEG-001 — Guided connection from Settings.** Settings → Integrations lists every integration with its state (not connected, connecting, connected, needs attention). An Owner or Admin connects one through the setup path in the table below: Vector generates every value the provider needs — this installation's request, event, OAuth redirect and sidebar URLs, least-privilege scopes, event subscriptions, a fresh signing secret, and any app package or plugin manifest — and either submits them through the provider's app-creation API or manifest flow, or prefills the provider's creation page, so the Admin never types or edits a scope, URL or manifest by hand; the only values an Admin copies are the credentials a provider shows once, which Vector stores encrypted (SEC-001). Each path ends with a live check: Vector calls the provider with the new credentials and asks the Admin to perform one real action (send `@Vector` a message, open a PR), and marks the integration connected only when that event arrives signed. ## Push further

Agents serve the whole job beyond today's up-and-comers: an accepted issue becomes a spec, a tested PR and a verified deployment, and the issue closes only when the evidence shows the user-facing problem is gone. A named person stays accountable at every step.

- **PUSH-001 — Issue to spec.** For an accepted issue, an agent drafts a spec: the problem in the reporter's and customers' words (CUST-001), the evidence (Spool errors, tickets, measurements), acceptance criteria written as checkable statements, the code areas likely affected (from repository search through GITHUB-006 or GITLAB-001), open questions and a test plan. The assignee edits and approves it; only an approved spec is attached as the issue's specification (PROJ-013) and used for delegation. *Acceptance:* a journey on a seeded bug with two Spool errors and a Zendesk ticket where the draft cites both, the assignee edits one criterion and approves, and the approved revision is attached. *Status:* missing.
- **PUSH-002 — Spec to tested PR.** When an issue with an approved spec is delegated (AGENT-001), the agent must return a PR whose tests map to the spec's acceptance criteria; Vector shows each criterion with the test that covers it, the CI result (GITHUB-005) and any criterion left uncovered, and refuses to mark the session done while required checks fail. A reviewer approves the PR in the code host. *Acceptance:* an agent fixture that returns a PR covering two of three criteria with passing checks, and the issue shows one uncovered criterion and the session not done. *Status:* missing.
- **PUSH-003 — Verified deployment.** After merge, Vector follows the merged commit through CI and deployments (RELEASE-002, CI-001) and records when it reached production; if it does not deploy within the team's expected window, the assignee is asked why. *Acceptance:* a merge fixture followed by a production deployment fixture that marks the issue deployed, and a second merge with no deployment that raises one question after the window. *Status:* missing.
- **PUSH-004 — Outcome verification before close.** After production deploy, a verification agent watches the issue's evidence for a team-set window (default 72 hours): linked Spool or Sentry error groups (rate and regressions in the new release), Uptime checks, HappyToHelp, Zendesk or Intercom tickets reopening, Penny requests and the spec's measurable criteria. It then proposes "Verified — problem gone" with the before and after evidence, or "Not fixed" with what it saw. The assignee confirms; only then does the issue reach a Verified state (or reopen). Issues without linked evidence say so instead of claiming verification. *Acceptance:* a journey with a linked Spool error group whose rate drops to zero after the deploy fixture, producing a Verified proposal that the assignee confirms, and a second issue whose error persists and is proposed Not fixed. *Status:* missing.
- **PUSH-005 — Customer follow-through.** When an issue with customer requests is verified, an agent drafts a reply for each linked ticket and request, in the customer's context and without private engineering details, and queues them for the support owner, who edits, approves or skips each; sends go through HappyToHelp, Zendesk, Intercom, Front or Penny, never directly from Vector, and are never sent twice. *Acceptance:* a verified issue with two tickets yields two drafts; approving one sends one fixture message, skipping the other sends none. *Status:* missing.
- **PUSH-006 — Blocked-work follow-up.** An agent watches dependencies, overdue work, stale reviews and unanswered questions; it identifies the missing decision or evidence, asks the accountable person a precise question in Vector or Slack, and revisits at the time they promise, within the team's reminder budget. ## Honest boundaries

Vector does not do these jobs, and buyers get them as follows (US-first):

- **Code hosting, CI runners and production deploys.** Vector links to GitHub, GitLab or Bitbucket and the buyer's CI and deploy platform (GitHub Actions, CircleCI, Vercel, Cloudflare); it never hosts repositories, runs builds or deploys.
- **Running coding agents.** Agents run in their providers' sandboxes (Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, Cursor, GitHub Copilot) or the buyer's own runners; Vector holds delegation state, context and evidence, never source checkouts or production credentials inside its Worker.
- **Customer feedback board, public roadmap and changelog.** Penny.
- **Support inbox, CRM, error tracking, uptime checks, paging.** HappyToHelp, Orbit, Spool, Uptime and Oncall, or Zendesk, Intercom, Front, Salesforce, HubSpot, Sentry, Datadog and PagerDuty through the integrations above.
- **Cross-functional work management for marketing, operations and agencies.** Not Vector's product (Asana, monday.com or ClickUp); Vector serves software delivery.
- **Wiki and design tools.** Vector has project documents and specs (PROJ-013) but is not a company wiki or design tool; it links Notion, Confluence, Google Drive and Figma.
- **Timesheets, billing and capitalised-labour reports.** Not provided; buyers use Harvest, Toggl or Clockify linked by issue identifier, and REPORT-005 exports for finance.
- **Enterprise service management (help-desk ITSM, change approval boards) and regulated validation.** Not provided; an issue status is not a legal approval or a compliance record. Buyers use Jira Service Management or ServiceNow alongside.
- **Perpetual parity with every incumbent field and app.** Imports report every omission, and transition syncs (DATA-016) are bounded; Vector does not promise ongoing two-way parity with Jira or Linear.
- **Buyer-branded mobile apps.** Members install Run Edit Run's store-listed apps and receive push through Run Edit Run's relay (MOBILE-001, MOBILE-002, MOBILE-004). A buyer who wants apps under their own name and developer accounts builds them from source and publishes them with their own Apple and Google push credentials; Vector does not publish to a buyer's store accounts for them (Linear does not offer branded apps either).
